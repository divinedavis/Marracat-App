# Marracat True Material

The badge on a bag means: **the listing names what the bag is made of, honestly.** Planned 2026-10-08 (label #4). The rules are in `catalog/material_badge.py` and the tests in `catalog/test_material_badge.py`.

## The rules

1. **The main material is named**, as one of:
   - **Real leather**: full-grain, top-grain, genuine or real leather, cowhide, calfskin, lambskin, goatskin, nappa, nubuck, suede, patent leather. The US FTC Guides for Select Leather and Imitation Leather Products (16 CFR Part 24) require imitation leather to be disclosed as such, so a listing that calls a bag leather is making a claim it can be held to. Plain "leather" counts only in the title, a material statement ("made of leather", "Material: leather") or a "leather bag" phrase — not "swap the leather strap" — and only when nothing in the listing says vegan, faux, PU or synthetic.
   - **A natural material**: cotton or canvas, linen, hemp, jute, raffia, straw, rattan, sisal, seagrass, wool, felt, cork, silk, denim, kente, mudcloth.
   - **A named alternative**: recycled nylon or polyester (ECONYL, REPREVE, rPET), cactus leather (Desserto), apple leather, mushroom leather (Mylo, mycelium), Piñatex, grape leather (Vegea), cork leather.
   - **An honestly named synthetic**: polyurethane (PU), nylon, polyester, neoprene. Not natural, but named: the badge rewards a bag that says what it is. Noted in the report.
2. **Fails:**

| Fails | Why |
|---|---|
| "Vegan leather", "faux leather", "leatherette", "leather-look", "pleather", "synthetic leather" with no material named | It doesn't say what the bag is. Naming the polymer (PU) or plant (cactus, apple) passes |
| PVC or vinyl not stated phthalate-free | EU REACH Annex XVII entry 51 (Reg. 2018/2005) restricts DEHP, DBP, BBP and DIBP — the usual PVC plasticisers — to 0.1% in articles; California Prop 65 lists them |
| No material named | Nothing to verify |

3. **Tier "Marracat True Material · Vegetable Tanned"**: leather stated vegetable-tanned, bark-tanned or chrome-free. Chromium VI is banned in leather articles in contact with skin above 3 mg/kg in the EU (REACH Annex XVII entry 47, Reg. 301/2014); it forms in chrome tanning, which vegetable tanning avoids.

Claims of absence ("PVC-free") are removed before the bans are looked for.

## What's judged

The bags department: handbags, totes, crossbodies, backpacks, wallets, pouches, travel bags. Bag-care products, charms, keychains, replacement straps and gift cards aren't judged. Read from each product's own title, description, tags and options; nothing is fetched.
