# Marracat Natural Fiber

The badge on a garment means: **the listing states what the garment is made of, and at least 90% of it is natural fibre.** The rules are in `catalog/fiber_badge.py` and the tests in `catalog/test_fiber_badge.py`. Label #5 of the plan the owner approved on 2026-10-08.

## What counts as natural fibre

| Natural | Not natural for this badge |
|---|---|
| Cotton (incl. organic, Pima, Supima, Egyptian), linen / flax, hemp, ramie | Viscose, rayon, modal, lyocell (Tencel), cupro, acetate, "bamboo" fabric |
| Wool (incl. merino, lambswool), alpaca, mohair, cashmere, camel, yak, angora | Polyester (incl. recycled), nylon / polyamide, acrylic, elastane / spandex / Lycra, polyurethane, polypropylene, PVC, metallic |
| Silk | |

**Why regenerated cellulose doesn't count.** Viscose, modal, lyocell and "bamboo" start as plants, but the pulp is dissolved in chemicals (carbon disulfide for viscose, NMMO for lyocell) and re-spun into a filament. The EU Textile Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011, Annex I, lists them as man-made fibres, separately from natural ones, and the US FTC's Textile Act rules (16 CFR 303.7) require them to be named as rayon or lyocell, never as "bamboo". This badge follows those definitions.

## The rules

1. **A composition must be stated.** Percentages ("95% cotton, 5% elastane"), "100% linen", or one fibre named as the material ("Fabric: organic cotton"). The common care-label abbreviations from EU 1007/2011 are read too ("89% rec.pes, 11% el", "72% ORG COT").
2. **At least 90% natural fibre.** The 10% allowance is for the elastane that gives jeans and tees stretch; it's noted on the product.
3. **The percentages must add up to the whole garment.** "60% cotton" with nothing else stated can't be judged.
4. **Fibre content that varies by colour fails.** Heather colourways of many blank tees switch to a cotton–polyester blend.
5. **Organic tier** ("Marracat Natural Fiber · Organic"): the listing says the fibre is organic (organic cotton, linen, hemp, wool, silk) or GOTS certified (Global Organic Textile Standard, which requires at least 70% certified organic fibre).

## What's judged

Clothing only. Shoes are mostly leather and rubber, and accessories mix metals, plastics and textiles, so neither is judged. Gift cards, stickers, prints, mugs, tote bags and digital patterns sold in a clothing shop aren't garments and aren't judged.

## What the badge doesn't claim

It's a check of what the listing says, not a lab test: a mislabelled garment would pass. Dyes and finishes are judged by Marracat Safe Fabric (`catalog/fabric_standard.md`), not here.
